What a German Schlüsseldienst does

Schlüsseldienst is the German word for a locksmith service. It is a private business, not a public authority: neither the police nor the fire brigade come out for a door that has simply fallen shut. You call a locksmith yourself and pay for the job on the spot.

Your landlord, hall of residence or Hausverwaltung (property manager) may hold a spare key, but those offices are only staffed during office hours. In the evening or at the weekend, a locksmith is normally the fastest way back into your flat.

Latched or locked?

Please check this before you call. If the door fell shut behind you, only the latch is holding it — that opening is usually quick and in most cases causes no damage. If you turned the key and the door is properly locked (in German: abgeschlossen), the bolts are extended. That takes longer, and additional labour costs are added on top of the callout price.

How a call-out works

1

You call

Tell us what happened: the door fell shut, the key snapped off, or the lock no longer turns. Give us the street and the district.

2

You get the price

We name the price and the expected arrival time before we leave the shop. A quote always comes before the work.

3

We drive out

Within the Aachen city area we are usually with you in about 30 minutes.

4

The door opens

We work as gently as possible and open without damage wherever the lock allows it. You pay on the spot.

Key lost rather than left inside?

Then the opening is only half the job. If the key could be traced to your address, a new lock cylinder is the safer choice. Our technician looks at the lock on site and names the price before fitting anything.

Frequently asked questions

What does "Schlüsseldienst" mean?

It is the German word for a locksmith service: a private company that opens doors, replaces locks, cuts keys and fits security hardware. You call it yourself and pay for the callout yourself.
